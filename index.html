GEOMETRY · INTRINSIC DIMENSION

2025–2026

HomID: a stress test for dimension estimators

Different estimators can give very different answers for the same data. HomID tests them on curved spaces whose true dimension is known.

Read the story →

TRANSFORMERS · IN-CONTEXT LEARNING

2024

Learning to grok from in-context examples

We use modular arithmetic to study when a transformer can combine learned skills and solve tasks it did not see during training.

Read the story →

LEARNABILITY · ANALYTICAL NETWORKS

2024

Solving arbitrary modular polynomials through architecture design.

An exact network can solve a task that ordinary training struggles with. We explore this gap by composing analytical arithmetic circuits.

Read the story →

MODEL DEPTH · CONTROLLED DATASETS

2024

Cellular Automata and Model Depth

Simple local rules generate patterns with very different behavior. We use these tasks to study the relationship between model depth and data complexity.

Read the story →

GROKKING · MEMORIZATION · ROBUSTNESS

2023–2024

Generalizing sub-circuits and how to find them.

We deliberately corrupt arithmetic labels to separate rule learning from memorization, then examine the neurons that support each behavior.

Read the story →

QUANTUM MATTER · GAUGE THEORY

2021–2023

Fractionalized holes in a one-dimensional gauge theory

Near full filling, a hole can separate two shifted quantum backgrounds. We study the resulting motion, entanglement, and emergent integrability.

03 / RESEARCH INTERESTS

A few questions I’m exploring.

Publications on Google Scholar ↗
3 research directions
01

EMERGENCE · MODULAR ARITHMETIC

How do neural networks
learn structure?

I use controlled modular arithmetic tasks to study emergent behavior in deep learning. Simple problems can reveal rich learning dynamics.

A closer look +

These tasks give us a controlled setting for asking how a model learns the structure of a problem, and how its behavior changes during training.

02

GEOMETRY · INTRINSIC DIMENSION

How many dimensions
does data really need?

I work on standards for evaluating intrinsic dimension. Manifolds from quantum information provide useful test cases with known structure.

A closer look +

Data can occupy a high-dimensional space while depending on far fewer degrees of freedom. Known manifolds help us test how well dimension estimators recover that structure.

03

QUANTUM INFORMATION · AI

Can AI help us solve
quantum problems?

I’m interested in using AI for quantum information and simulation, including efficient quantum algorithms and the structure of physical data.

A closer look +

This is a direction I’m excited to explore: using learning methods to understand physical data and find efficient implementations of quantum algorithms.

I’m a PhD student at the University of Maryland, College Park, working at the intersection of physics and artificial intelligence.
