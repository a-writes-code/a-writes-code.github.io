Different estimators can give very different answers for the same data. HomID tests them on curved spaces whose true dimension is known.

03 / RESEARCH INTERESTS

A few questions I’m exploring.

Publications on Google Scholar ↗
3 research directions
01

STRUCTURE OF LANGUAGE · TREES

How do we experimentally
probe the structure of
language?

I’m interested in developing trainable models that can approximate language.

A closer look +

How can we better understand what properties of data influence learning dynamics, representations and mechanisms? We introduce tree-grams, which serve as controlled approximators of language. We see that data from such generated models can throw light on practical situations like hyper-parameter tuning and pre-training to finetuning transfer.

02

GEOMETRY · INTRINSIC DIMENSION

How many dimensions
does data really need?

I work on standards for evaluating intrinsic dimension. Manifolds from quantum information provide useful test cases with known structure.

A closer look +

Data can occupy a high-dimensional space while depending on far fewer degrees of freedom. Intrinsic dimension estimators need stricter protocols and targetted stressors in evaluation benchmarks. Manifolds from quantum information (or classic differential geometry, based on your taste), can help highlight the role of anisotropy. This can also help design better estimators.

03

SYNTHETIC DATA · MODULAR ARITHMETIC

How do neural networks
learn structure?

I use controlled modular arithmetic tasks to study emergent behavior in deep learning. Simple problems can reveal rich learning dynamics.

A closer look +

These tasks give us a controlled setting for asking how a model learns the structure of a problem, and how its behavior changes during training.
