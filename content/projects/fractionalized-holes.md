# How can a missing particle become a moving boundary?

Removing a particle from an interacting system need not create a simple empty spot. The surrounding quantum state can reorganize, giving the missing particle a different description and different dynamics.

## A hole as a domain wall

We study fermions coupled to a one-dimensional Z₂ gauge field. Near full filling, two degenerate backgrounds differ by a lattice translation. A hole can act as a boundary between them.

This *domain-wall* description explains how isolated holes can be deconfined in this setting, even though the original fermionic particles are confined. The distinction depends on which background and excitations we consider.

## Follow the dynamics

We combine analytical arguments with numerical calculations of hole spreading and entanglement. In the strong string-tension limit, the effective description has correlated hopping: a hole moves by two sites, subject to the occupancy of the intermediate site.

This limit connects to an integrable model. We also examine how short-range interactions change the low-energy behavior. The results depend on filling and interaction regime; deconfinement of isolated holes near full filling is not a statement about every state at finite hole density.

> **The takeaway**
>
> The right description of an excitation depends on its background. A missing particle can behave as a domain wall.

## A few useful terms

- **Domain wall.** A boundary between two distinct ordered backgrounds.
- **Deconfinement.** The absence of an energy cost that grows without bound as the relevant excitations separate.
- **Integrability.** A special mathematical structure that constrains a system’s dynamics.
